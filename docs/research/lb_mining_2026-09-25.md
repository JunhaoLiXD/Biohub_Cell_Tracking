# LB mining of the ≥0.956 cohort — 2026-09-25

Zero GPU, zero submissions. Author: Claude Code. Method and every claim below are reproducible from
the authenticated Kaggle CLI.

## Method

1. Full authenticated leaderboard download, **3894 teams**, snapshot `2026-09-25T01:45:42`.
   The CSV carries `TeamMemberUserNames`, which resolves the team-name ≠ username problem that would
   otherwise make author matching guesswork.
2. Took every team scoring **≥ 0.956** → 77 teams, **110 distinct usernames**.
3. Queried `kaggle kernels list --user <name> --competition biohub-cell-tracking-during-development`
   for each of the 110. (Competition-wide paging via `kernels list -p N` is unreliable — page 1
   returned 2 rows, page 2 returned 20 — so per-user queries were used instead.)
4. Pulled the interesting candidates and diffed their configurations against each other, against our
   parent `repro_059`, and against `x138`.

Result: **28 public notebooks** from the ≥0.956 cohort. Hashes for everything pulled:
`public_notebook_archive/SHA256SUMS_frontier947_series.txt`.

### Why this matters

**We have already submitted x138** (`56535761`, pending). `readmit-v1` is that same pipeline **minus
one mechanism**. Running it would give a matched, single-mechanism A/B on the Public LB — the
cleanest causal isolation available to this project at any point in its history, at roughly 0.26 GPU
hours, using only checkpoints we already mount.

**The hypothesis it would test, stated as a hypothesis and not as evidence:** thtennant sits at
rank 37 / 0.959 without the head; anvithpothula sits at rank 67 / 0.956 with it. That is suggestive
that the V1284 head is not the win, and may be a loss. It is **not** evidence: the two are different
teams with different unshared work, thtennant published eleven notebooks and we cannot know which
one produced 0.959, and a team's authenticated score never authenticates a particular notebook they
published. The A/B is what would settle it, and it settles it *for our own pipeline*, which is the
only thing that matters.

### A second, free finding from x138's own comments

The x138 author documents that their V1284 patch and readmit's patch **anchor on the same line**,
and that in an earlier ordering theirs ran first, readmit's anchor count went to 0, the exception
was swallowed as non-fatal, and the low-detection dump was silently never written — every movie
logged "gap filler idle". They fixed it by reordering. This is the fourth independent instance in
this project's recon of the same failure mode: **present, correct, fully-wired machinery that
silently does nothing at runtime.** It is also, to their credit, evidence that this author checks
their own logs.

Their self-reported head provenance is more detailed than we previously credited (still
self-reported, still unauditable): trained on a 20-movie TRAIN capture, 4,136 detection↔GT pairs,
held out by movie, moving centres closer to truth by 10.4–10.8 % pooled with 16 of 20 movies
improving — and they note a 4-movie version of the same head was 14.9 % *worse*.

## Note on `tight_um`, which bears on a claim we withdrew

Two independent things showed up:

- `thtennant` built a **dedicated single-variable notebook** for `MOTION_RELINK_TIGHT_UM` 5.5 → 6.0.
  A rank-37 team considered this lever worth isolating.
- Our own parent `repro_059` sets `BIOHUB_MOTION_RELINK_TIGHT_UM = '6.0'` **statically**, and its
  embedded PP-sweep then re-selects `tight55` at runtime. The frontier947 family sets 5.5 statically
  and disables the sweep (`BIOHUB_VALIDATOR_ENABLE=0`), arriving at the same effective 5.5 by a
  different route.

Neither of these restores the withdrawn claim. We still do not know the *direction* of the effect,
and we still have no score for any tight60 arm. What they do establish is that the lever is live
enough that a stronger team isolated it deliberately.
